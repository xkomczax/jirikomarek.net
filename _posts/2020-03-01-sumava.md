Deep forests. Vanished hamlets. Treacherous bogs. Wild nature. The Šumava Mountains in Southern Bohemia are a feast for the eyes.

For centuries, the Šumava Mountains (the Bohemian Forest, German Böhmerwald) were an almost uninhabited place. Just a few trails serving merchants for trade between Bohemia and Bavaria cut through the infinite woods. Hostile to humans, friendly to wild animals.

The biggest change came at the end of the 18th century with the Schwarzenberk canal, designed for floating timber. Massive deforestation and transformation of the virgin forest into the industrial-growth forest were followed by drainage works.

Nature got a second chance, for the wrong reason, after the expulsion of the German-speaking population from the borderlands after the Second World War and the closing of the borders during the Cold War.

Nowadays, it is protected as Šumava National Park, and many areas are left to natural processes.
